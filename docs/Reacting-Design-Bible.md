THE REACTING MANIFESTO
Healthcare teams should spend their time improving care—not chasing information.
Dental practices are full of highly trained people doing work that should not require their time.
Owners interrupt their day to ask whether something was ordered.
Managers search through emails, spreadsheets and messages to understand what is happening.
Head nurses count cupboards, chase supplier replies and repeat information already shared elsewhere.
Nurses look for products, check whether stock has arrived and ask who is responsible.
Reception teams pass messages between people because the answer is not visible.
The problem is not that people are failing.
The problem is that operational information is fragmented, delayed and dependent on memory.
Reacting exists to change that.
________________________________________
We believe time inside a healthcare practice is too valuable to waste.
Every unnecessary check has a cost.
Every repeated conversation has a cost.
Every spreadsheet updated twice has a cost.
Every person waiting for an answer has a cost.
Every manager completing work that software could organise has a cost.
Every owner pulled away from patients or business development has a cost.
Operational inefficiency is not only frustrating.
It consumes staff time, increases employment costs and prevents capable people from focusing on more valuable work.
Reacting is built to return that time to the practice.
________________________________________
We believe owners should see without having to ask.
Practice owners should not need to walk through stockrooms, search emails or question several members of staff to understand what is happening.
They should be able to see:
•	what the practice holds;
•	what needs attention;
•	what has been ordered;
•	what has arrived;
•	what remains outstanding;
•	what the practice is spending;
•	where work is delayed;
•	and who is responsible.
Visibility should not require another meeting.
Control should not depend on constant interruption.
The owner should be free to focus on patients, leadership, growth and the future of the practice.
________________________________________
We believe managers should manage—not chase.
Practice managers often become the connection between every person and every process.
They answer questions.
They search for information.
They follow up suppliers.
They check whether staff completed tasks.
They reconstruct what happened from emails, messages and memory.
This is not management.
It is operational recovery.
Reacting gives managers one place to see work, responsibility, progress and exceptions.
Their time should be spent improving the practice—not repeatedly rebuilding information that should already exist.
________________________________________
We believe head nurses and ordering leads deserve a simpler working day.
The person responsible for stock is often expected to manage it around clinical duties.
They are asked to:
•	remember what is running low;
•	count materials;
•	compare suppliers;
•	check product codes;
•	identify substitutes;
•	follow up missing deliveries;
•	update spreadsheets;
•	and explain purchasing decisions afterwards.
This work is important, but it should not be unnecessarily difficult.
Reacting turns scattered tasks into one clear workflow.
The system remembers the detail, keeps the evidence and shows what happens next.
The user remains in control without carrying the entire process in their head.
________________________________________
We believe nurses should be able to act quickly and confidently.
Clinical teams should not waste time searching cupboards, asking where products are stored or wondering whether stock has already been ordered.
Everyday actions should be simple.
Take an item.
Update a quantity.
Complete a stock check.
Record a delivery.
Report a problem.
The system should fit around clinical work rather than compete with it.
Good operational software should make the correct action easier than ignoring it.
________________________________________
We believe reception teams should not become human notification systems.
Reception is already interrupted constantly.
They should not need to pass messages such as:
“Can you ask whether this was ordered?”
“Can you check whether the delivery arrived?”
“Can you tell the manager the supplier called?”
Information should reach the correct person directly.
Status should be visible.
Responsibility should be clear.
Reception time should remain focused on patients and the front of the practice.
________________________________________
We believe every role should see what matters to them.
Owners need oversight.
Managers need coordination.
Head nurses need control of stock and ordering.
Nurses need quick, practical actions.
Finance teams need reliable records.
Multi-site groups need consistency.
Reacting should not show everyone the same complexity.
It should give each person the right information at the right time.
________________________________________
We believe practices should save money by working better—not by cutting corners.
The lowest quoted price is not always the best decision.
A different brand may be unsuitable.
A smaller pack may appear cheaper.
A delayed item may create another emergency order.
A delivery charge may remove the saving.
An expired product may never be used.
A duplicate order may remain unnoticed.
Reacting connects product identity, supplier offers, purchasing, delivery and history so practices can make better decisions.
The aim is not simply to spend less.
It is to spend deliberately.
________________________________________
We believe operational savings include staff time.
Practices often measure the cost of products but ignore the cost of managing them.
Ten minutes spent checking a cupboard.
Fifteen minutes finding an invoice.
Several phone calls to compare prices.
Repeated follow-ups for an incomplete order.
A manager correcting information at month end.
Individually these tasks appear small.
Across a team, a month and several practices, they become expensive.
Reacting makes hidden operational work visible and reduces the need for it.
________________________________________
We believe information should arrive before the decision that depends on it.
A stock warning after the product has run out is too late.
A price discrepancy after the invoice is paid is too late.
A missing delivery discovered on the day of treatment is too late.
A compliance expiry noticed after the deadline is too late.
A staffing conflict found on the morning of the shift is too late.
Reacting moves information forward.
The system should help people act before a problem becomes urgent, expensive or disruptive.
________________________________________
We believe every workflow should have one visible owner.
When everyone is responsible, nobody is responsible.
Every important operational task should show:
•	who owns it;
•	what needs completing;
•	when it is due;
•	what has already happened;
•	and what remains outstanding.
Accountability should be factual, not personal.
The purpose is not to blame people.
It is to prevent uncertainty and miscommunication.
________________________________________
We believe software should remove conversations—not create more of them.
People should not need to ask:
“Did somebody order it?”
“Has it arrived?”
“Which supplier did we use?”
“Why was this brand changed?”
“Who adjusted the quantity?”
“Where is the invoice?”
“Has the audit been completed?”
The answer should already exist.
Reacting does not replace useful human communication.
It removes repetitive communication caused by missing information.
________________________________________
We believe the practice should own its process and its data.
Reacting should not force a practice into one supplier, one rigid workflow or one way of operating.
Practices should be able to work with the suppliers they already trust.
They should be able to export their information.
They should be able to adapt the system to their locations, responsibilities and approval process.
The software supports the practice.
The practice does not work for the software.
________________________________________
We believe implementation should lead to independence.
Good support helps users become confident.
It should not make them dependent on continuous training calls.
A new practice should understand:
•	where to begin;
•	what matters first;
•	what can wait;
•	what to do next;
•	and where to find help.
The product should become easier through use.
The goal is not to complete an implementation project.
The goal is for the practice to begin working differently.
________________________________________
We believe operations should connect.
Inventory affects purchasing.
Purchasing affects invoices.
Invoices affect budgets.
Budgets affect profitability.
Appointments affect material demand.
Staff availability affects service delivery.
Laboratory work affects treatment timelines.
Compliance affects every part of the practice.
These are not separate businesses.
They belong to one connected operation.
Inventory and procurement are the first Reacting module.
They are not the final destination.
________________________________________
We are building the operational platform behind modern healthcare practices.
A platform where:
•	information is visible before somebody asks;
•	routine work requires less time;
•	decisions are supported by evidence;
•	responsibilities are clear;
•	processes do not leave when an employee leaves;
•	managers can focus on improving the practice;
•	owners can lead without constantly checking;
•	staff can complete tasks without unnecessary complexity;
•	and healthcare teams can spend more time on work that genuinely requires people.
Reacting exists to make healthcare operations calmer, clearer, faster and more connected.
Not through more administration.
Through less of it.
Not by replacing people.
By giving their time back.
________________________________________
The Reacting Promise
We will build software that respects the value of people’s time.
We will remove unnecessary work before adding new features.
We will connect workflows before creating more dashboards.
We will show the next action before displaying more information.
We will make responsibility visible without creating blame.
We will help practices spend wisely without lowering standards.
We will never confuse complexity with capability.
We will measure success not by how much software a practice uses, but by how much unnecessary work it no longer needs to do.
Reacting gives healthcare teams more time to do work that matters.
